First of all open source is about people, like every project out there. And as such open source projects are basically the nightmare of every project manager. You have a highly distributed team - across many time zones and cultures. That in itself is a challenge. But you mostly don't have money. So you can not just make exchange visits or other team building efforts. Most developers will never meet in person. Communication is often asynchronous and hard. Most developers voluntarily contribute to open source with highly varying degrees of time investment. One of your leading developers could stop working on the project any day. Since your team is so distributed knowledge sharing is also tough so you might not know what's going on in his/her code. You also have some drive by commits at the same time - people that just come by, implement one feature or bug and then move on. So sharing ideas hard, your team barely knows each other and changes all the time and you can not really rely on any time investment from anyone. Basically a nightmare scenario. Still open projects are successful. Why? Motivation. Passion. All these people come together to work on a project because they really want to. Because they like the project. They make this whole situation work. Be aware of these factors when you work on open source. People from another culture might communicate differently or be offended by things you might see as normal. Everyone is doing this in their free time, so be nice and a bit thankful. And the only thing that keeps them doing this is some kind of motivation. So try to keep people engaged and motivated. Try to give quick feedback on issues and especially pull requests.
